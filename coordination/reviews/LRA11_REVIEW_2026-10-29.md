## 1. Independently verified

Both corrections the return makes to the **manager's own packet** are right,
and the packet was wrong:

| Claim | Check |
|---|---|
| Raising the divider 10× gives **19.7 µA**, not the packet's ~14 µA | **Confirmed, 19.7 µA.** The packet scaled linearly. That is wrong: the unpowered 3V3 rail's leakage load is roughly a fixed 8,083 Ω derived from the measured state, and it does not scale with the divider. 20× gives 10.1 µA, still failing the 8 µA cold limit |
| Lifting the external clamp puts GP2 at **2.622 V** | **Confirmed exactly.** 4.669 V across 3,757 + 22k + 33k is 79.46 µA, and 79.46 µA × 33 kΩ = 2.622 V |

The parked model reproducing the measured state at the fitted divider
(140.81 µA, 1.042 V, 109.23 µA) is what earns the scaled cases their weight.
That is the right way to build this and it is why the corrections are credible.

## 2. The most valuable result is not the part

**The clamp-topology direction is dead, permanently, on every branch.** The
diode that back-feeds is the **RP2040's own pin-to-IOVDD ESD structure**, which
cannot be removed. Lifting the external Schottky raises GP2 to 2.622 V and
forward-biases the internal one *harder* — the back-feed survives at a higher
rail voltage.

Because the clamp reference and the logic reference are the same rail, no clamp
value or topology escapes this. **It generalises to rows 1–5 of
`REV_A_INTERFACES` §6:** none of those can be closed by clamp changes on any
branch. It also re-derives §3.2's "isolation must be upstream of the divider"
rule from measurement instead of assertion.

That closes a solution class for good and is worth more than the part number.

## 3. R11-REV-1 — requirement 7 is not met, and the repo answer differs from the return's assumption

The return asks whether supervisor BAD drops B5. **It does not — and the
reason is not the one it expected.**

1. **MAX_ON is not implemented in Rev A.** `X8` is an unpopulated placeholder
   and LRA-06 Q6 established that `HOLD_N` reaches only a test point, never the
   HOLD path. **Today BAD has no path to QP or B5 at all.**
2. **Even with MAX_ON implemented**, `MAX_ON_CUTOFF.md` inserts `QS` only into
   the SINK-to-QH-collector connection. `ARM_RESET = BAD OR EXPIRED OR HOLD_N`
   therefore gates **only the firmware HOLD branch**. `QI`, the direct ignition
   path, sits in parallel on SINK and is untouched, so **BAD cannot drop B5
   while ignition is present.**

So the proposed fail-open-in-hardware claim does not hold as written.

**But the scheme is better than that makes it sound, and the return undersells
itself.** B5 alive is *exactly* the condition "board powered from the vehicle":
B5 dies only when QP opens, which needs both QI and QH off. So a B5-fed LED
makes the switch **track the board's own power state by construction**:

- Parked, key out — QP off, B5 dead, LED dark, **switch open.** Correct, and
  this is the case the whole task exists for.
- USB-only bench work — B5 dead, board alive through VBUS, **switch open.**
  Correct and arguably a bonus.
- EXPIRED with HOLD stuck, ignition absent — QS opens, B5 dies, **switch
  open.** Correct, once MAX_ON exists.
- Ignition on, BAD asserted — B5 stays up and the switch stays closed. But in
  that state the rails are up and the board is powered, so there is no
  back-feed into a dead rail to prevent.

**For the electrical lead, not resolved here:** whether requirement 7 should be
reworded to "open whenever the switched rail is down", which this satisfies by
construction, or whether a literal A5-domain PGOOD gate is additionally wanted.
The return's own proposed fix — a second series N-FET in the LED path gated by
PGOOD — is the right shape if the literal reading is kept.

## 4. Availability — resolved, and better than reported

The return flagged contradictory lifecycle data as its main reason for
specifying a fallback in detail. Checked: **no obsolescence notice exists.**
DigiKey lists both orderable codes as **Active** with real stock — roughly
5,486 of `ASSR-601JV-000E` and 1,492 of `ASSR-601JV-500E`, the latter at an
18-week standard lead time. The "obsolete" flag the return saw most likely
belongs to the sibling **ASSR-601J**, a different part, whose distributor pages
do show out-of-stock.

**A detail the return did not draw out, and it strengthens the case:** the
datasheet is titled *"ASSR-601JV, ASSR-601JT — Automotive Solid State Relay"*.
The JV is the **automotive-qualified** variant. The Panasonic AQY221N2S
fallback was rejected partly for stating verbatim that it is *"not designed for
automotive use"*; the recommended part is the opposite of that, in a vehicle
application. Worth stating explicitly in `B2_ISOLATION.md`.

## 5. The one load-bearing claim still unverified

**The leakage specification rests on a reading of Broadcom's table
convention**, not on an explicit sentence. The argument is that the DC table
header says minimum/maximum specifications hold over recommended operating
conditions, that the `I_O(OFF)` row carries no temperature override, and that
the withstand row immediately above it does say `T_A = 25 °C` — so the absence
is meaningful.

That is a careful and probably correct reading. It is still an inference from
formatting, and **this exact distinction is what disqualified both the CPC1017N
and the ADG5412F.** The entire recommendation rests on this one point.

**Required before integration, not before further design work:** confirm the
`I_O(OFF)` maximum applies over the full temperature range, from an explicit
datasheet statement, an application note, or Broadcom directly. If it turns out
to be 25 °C-only, the part falls to the same objection as the rejected
candidates and the search reopens.

## 6. Accepted without change

The rejections are sound and well-reasoned: the Omron G6K-2F-Y on its 70 °C
rating against an 85 °C spec point and an initial-room-temperature IR figure;
the CPC1017N on 25 °C-only leakage; the discrete FET on needing the same charge
pump that killed the ADG5412F, or putting a body diode in the signal path.
`check_b2_isolation.py` passes 24 checks, stdlib only.

The sequencing correction is right and the packet should have anticipated it:
requirement 6 wants the switch open before HOLD releases, and the 0.5 ms
`T_OFF` maximum sets a real delay. 5 ms isolate-then-release on shutdown and a
20 ms settle on startup are reasonable; LRA-05's firmware owner should confirm
they fit the shutdown budget.
